Meet the machines NASA left behind

NASA has left machines on the Moon, on Mars and in deep space. Some went quiet. Some are still talking. Spin each one around, then let it tell you its story.

Sources, AI disclosure and accuracy

AI disclosure: This project was built with AI assistance. The code, the story text and the shapes of the built-in 3D models were generated with Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic, and then directed and reviewed by the project team. The page uses no NASA logos or other NASA branding. Please check facts against the NASA sources below.

NASA resources used (facts checked against these pages):

  • NASA/JPL, “The Apollo Experiment That Keeps on Giving” (2019): Apollo 11 mirrors and the Moon drifting away 3.8 cm a year. nasa.gov
  • NASA Science, Surveyor 3, and the NASA image “Man and Machine” (Apollo 12 landed about 180 m / 600 ft away; camera and scoop returned): science.nasa.gov/surveyor-3 · Man and Machine · NASA NSSDCA
  • NASA/JPL release on the end of the Opportunity mission, 13 Feb 2019 (45 km, last signal 10 June 2018): reprinted by ScienceDaily
  • NASA announcement on the end of the Ingenuity mission, 25 Jan 2024 (72 flights, about 17 km): reported by Space.com
  • NASA/JPL Voyager one light-day milestone, 18 Nov 2026 and Golden Record (55 languages): EarthSky, citing NASA
  • Other stories (Apollo 17 rover, Sojourner, Spirit, Pioneer 10) follow NASA mission histories at science.nasa.gov, nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov and history.nasa.gov/alsj.

Tools and code: Three.js r128 and its OrbitControls and GLTFLoader add-ons (MIT license) for the 3D viewer; the Inter typeface (SIL Open Font License) from Google Fonts; Claude (Anthropic) for code and text. The visual style was inspired by a web-design reference chosen by the team, and none of its images or code are used.

Stories are written in the machines’ own voices, but the facts come from NASA mission records. Good places to check or go deeper: NASA Science (science.nasa.gov), the NASA Space Science Data Coordinated Archive (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov), the Apollo Lunar Surface Journal (history.nasa.gov/alsj), NASA’s Mars rover pages (mars.nasa.gov), and the Voyager mission site (voyager.jpl.nasa.gov). Dot positions on the maps are only approximate. “Still talking” means a signal or instrument was still working when this was written. Check the mission pages for the latest.
